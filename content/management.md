High Output Management by Andrew Grove has shaped how I think about management: my contribution is the output of my team. I focus my time where it can increase that output, especially on hiring, training, and removing constraints on the critical path. I give engineers a clear direction and show how their work connects to business and shareholder value, then give them the autonomy to decide how to deliver.

When I took over the team, communication was weak. Engineers often did not know what happened to the features they built, and leadership had little visibility into their work. I introduced daily written updates, which AI summarizes and sends to leadership each day. Engineers now see feedback on their work, and leadership sees what they contribute. This has improved motivation and helped restore a high-agency engineering culture. That loop is central to how I manage.

Three engineers on my team were promoted within one year. I put people on the critical path, give them the support they need to grow, and make sure their work is seen.

7 Powers influences how I approach products. I look for opportunities to build process power and data flywheels: ways for what we learn through operating a product to improve it over time. I want that accumulated knowledge to create a lasting advantage.
